The Best Burritos in Charleston

Charleston is better known for shrimp and grits than for anything wrapped in a tortilla, but the burrito scene has quietly gotten good. These are the seven we would send a friend to, each one known for a different style.

The Holy City

Seven Burritos Worth the Trip

01Best overall

Torres Superette

843 Rutledge Avenue · Wagener Terrace

A neighborhood corner store with a kitchen in the back, and the burrito locals argue is the best in town. The carne asada is the order: a heavy flour tortilla packed with grilled steak, beans and seasoned rice that is good enough to eat on its own. There is usually a wait and seating is limited, so plan on taking it to go and eating it in Hampton Park a few blocks away.

CARNE ASADA · CORNER STORE · TAKEOUT
02Best pressed

The Press

221 Coming Street · Cannonborough-Elliottborough

A short menu built around one idea: a California-style burrito pressed on the grill until the tortilla turns crisp. Inside is cheese, french fries, avocado and pico de gallo with grilled steak or chicken, and there is a bean version for vegetarians. The fries are what set it apart. It runs as a pickup counter, so order ahead and collect it on the way to wherever you are headed.

STEAK · CHICKEN · CALIFORNIA STYLE
03Best breakfast burrito
Best Breakfast Burrito

Sweet Palm Coffee & Food

471 King Street · Upper King

The only morning burrito on this list, and the best one downtown. Sweet Palm is a King Street cafe with a real kitchen, and the burrito is made in house when you order it. The classic is eggs and cheese in a warm flour tortilla. The loaded version adds crispy potatoes, steak, cheddar, avocado and pico with a green sauce on the side, and the potatoes stay crisp inside the wrap, which is where most breakfast burritos fall apart.

Breakfast is served all day, the same fillings come as a bowl, and the combo pairs a personal-size burrito with an iced latte. See the full menu or order ahead for pickup. It is also one of our favorite cafes in Charleston.

EGG & CHEESE · STEAK & POTATO · ALL-DAY BREAKFAST · COFFEE
04Best smothered

Rancho Lewis

Upper Peninsula

Pitmaster John Lewis grew up on the cooking of El Paso and New Mexico, and this is his restaurant for it. The burrito here is a knife-and-fork plate, covered in red or green Hatch chile sauce and melted cheese. Ask for it Christmas style to get both sauces. The flour tortillas are made in house, and the smoked meats come from the same hands behind Lewis Barbecue.

HATCH CHILE · SMOTHERED · SIT-DOWN
05Best seafood

Semilla

218 President Street · Westside

A taco shop that started as a food truck and brought its Lowcountry habits with it. The shrimp burrito is the one to get: a twelve-inch flour tortilla with aromatic rice, black beans, queso fresco, cabbage and radish, finished with chipotle salsa, lime aioli and pickles. It is lighter and brighter than most of this list, and the right choice on a hot day.

SHRIMP · CHIPOTLE SALSA · LIME AIOLI
06Best taqueria

El Pincho Taco

616B Meeting Street · North Central

A family-run taqueria serving fillings that are hard to find elsewhere in the city. Order the campechano, which mixes carne asada with crumbled chorizo, or the cochinita pibil, pork slow-roasted Yucatán style. The salsa bar matters here, so take your time with it.

CAMPECHANO · COCHINITA PIBIL · SALSA BAR
07Best smoked

Home Team BBQ

1205 Ashley River Road · West Ashley

A barbecue joint is an odd place for a burrito until you try this one. Home Team wraps its smoked meat in a flour tortilla and treats it like any other plate on the menu, which means the smoke does the work. It is the pick for anyone in the group who would rather be eating pulled pork anyway.

SMOKED MEAT · BBQ BURRITO · PATIO
How We Picked

One Style Per Spot

Every place on this list is here for a different kind of burrito, so the ranking is less about better and worse than about what you are in the mood for.
